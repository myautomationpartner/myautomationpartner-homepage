Set up business social accounts the right way.

This guide explains what Facebook, Instagram, website chat, and paid ad accounts are for, who should own them, and how to avoid messy duplicate accounts later.

  • ✓A business email and phone that can receive security codes.

  • ✓A Facebook Page for the business.

Facebook requirements

Facebook business Pages are managed by real personal Facebook profiles. The profile is the private login; the Page is the public business presence.

Important

  • You need a real personal Facebook account to create or manage a business Page.
  • The personal profile is not the business Page and does not replace the business Page.
  • Customers see the business Page, not the owner’s personal profile, when the business posts.
  • Do not create a fake personal profile. Facebook may restrict or remove fake accounts.

Create or confirm the Page

  1. Log in with the real personal profile that will manage the business.
  2. Create a Page if one does not already exist.
  3. Add business name, logo, website, phone, address or service area, and description.
  4. Add another trusted admin if the business needs backup access.

Instagram and Meta Business Suite

For business use, Instagram should be a professional account and should ideally be connected to your Facebook Page.

Instagram

  • Use an Instagram Business account when possible.
  • If the account is personal, switch it to a professional account before connecting it to business tools.
  • Use a business-owned email and turn on two-factor authentication.

Meta Business Suite

  • Connect the Instagram account to the Facebook Page.
  • Keep the owner or trusted admin as the person who can approve permissions.
  • Add a payment method only if paid ads or boosted posts are part of the plan.

If you are not sure

Do not guess or create duplicate accounts. It is usually better to recover and clean up the correct account than start over with a second one.

If you do not have accounts yet

If accounts already exist

Check what is already out there before creating anything new.

Look for duplicates

  • Old Facebook Pages with the same or similar business name.
  • Instagram accounts created by past employees, agencies, or volunteers.
  • Profiles customers may already be following.

Check ownership

  • Confirm who has full control or admin access.
  • Remove people who no longer work with the business.
  • Make sure recovery email and phone numbers still work.

Recover before replacing

If the right account already exists, try to recover and clean it up before making a duplicate account.

Website chat and paid ads

These are optional at first, but they are useful when the business is ready for website messages or promoted content.

Website chat

  • You need access to your website builder, website admin, or developer.
  • A chat provider will usually provide a small website script.
  • The script is usually added before the closing body tag of the website.
  • Website chat should ask visitors for name and email so conversations become real contacts.

Paid ads and boosted posts

  • You only need this if you plan to run paid promotions.
  • Confirm the business has a Meta ad account and current payment method.
  • Decide who can approve budgets.
  • No one should launch paid ads without the business owner’s approval.

Security best practices

Use these rules for every account before connecting tools or giving anyone access.

Protect access

  • Keep the business owner as the primary admin.
  • Use business-owned email addresses when possible.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication.

Keep records clean

  • Keep recovery email and phone numbers current.
  • Remove old employees or vendors who no longer need access.
  • Avoid using one employee’s personal login as the only way into a business account.

Never share passwords

Do not send passwords through email, text, or chat. Use official login, invitation, and approval screens whenever possible.

  • ✓Paid ad budget only if ads are part of the plan.
